# Sources for international song expansion

Research checked 2026-11-15. This assesses source capabilities; no candidate import or live preview audit was executed. The proposed collection strategy is an inference from those capabilities, not a measured acceptance yield.

## Practical automation constraints

MusicBrainz requires an identifying User-Agent. Its published shared-IP limit is one request per second, and overload can return 503. Use one limiter across workers, caching, resumability and retry/backoff; the project's 1.1-second spacing is a suitable starting point. Its API documentation describes free noncommercial use and points commercial users to commercial plans. [API rules](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API), [rate limiting](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API/Rate_Limiting).

Browse release groups/releases for reviewed artist IDs, select supported original official releases, and fetch tracklists with recording IDs. MusicBrainz recording search's `country` field is the country of a release, not performer nationality. Retain artist identity and market evidence separately. [API](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API), [search fields](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API/Search/RecordingSearch).

For the first international expansion, prioritize existing missing matches and reviewed hit/single lists. Use original album tracks to fill remaining year gaps only when there is recognition evidence. UK charts are an additive second market, not sufficient evidence of multilingual breadth. Additional regional chart adapters should be implemented only after verifying their archive coverage and access conditions; this research did not establish German, French, Japanese or Latin chart bulk interfaces.

## Recording identity and year gates

Different performances, edits and mixes may be separate recordings. Ordinary remastering usually retains the underlying recording, so neither reject every remaster nor accept one solely on its title. Route unclear compilation tracks, later studio performances, live versions and remixes for review. [MusicBrainz recording guidelines](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/Style/Recording).

Use matching ISRCs as identity evidence alongside full artist credits, duration and release/version metadata. ISRC's embedded year is its assignment year, so it must never determine the card's release year. Even a formally valid ISRC can be erroneous. [IFPI ISRC structure](https://isrc.ifpi.org/isrc-standard/isrc-structure).

The original release year must belong to the chosen artist's specific recording; a chart year or a modern compilation date cannot silently become that year. Store evidence URLs, source dates, release/recording IDs, raw year precision and the reason for acceptance. Automatic acceptance should require a strong recording link, compatible release evidence and a fresh successful preview check. Ambiguous title/artist matches need human review.

## Pilot that resolves the uncertainties

Use about 200 candidates across deficient decades, split between existing missing matches and genuinely new discoveries. Audit every provisional automatic acceptance in this pilot. Report net novel recordings, dates corrected, verified previews, automatic acceptance rate, review minutes and per-decade yield. Calculate the full candidate budget from the observed yield; do not promise hundreds of additions simply because a source has thousands of entries.

Probe Deezer search, track metadata and actual audio responses from the same environment as the intended pipeline. Cache metadata and check timestamps, not expiring preview URLs or downloaded audio. Treat transient failures separately from confirmed misses. If a source cannot be accessed, report the blocker and use an independently supported source; do not lower identity checks to meet a numerical quota.
