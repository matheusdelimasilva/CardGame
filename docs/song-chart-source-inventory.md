## Institutional archive with wider primary coverage, access not established

The [Arquivo Edgard Leuenroth / UNICAMP IBOPE inventory](https://ael.ifch.unicamp.br/pf-ael/2022-09/inventario_ibope.pdf), printed p. 137, describes series **IBOPE PD 001–037**: 1,987 weekly/monthly record-sales surveys in 37 volumes, dated **1959–1961 and 1966–1978**. It distinguishes compactos simples, compactos duplos and LPs, and markets including Rio, São Paulo and Recife. This is promising for 1960–61 and the late 1960s/1970s, but the inventory is a finding aid, not a machine-readable set of ranked song rows.

A concrete legacy scan URL for the 1967 São Paulo volume is:

https://segall.ifch.unicamp.br/documentos_digitalizados/ibope/ibope_opiniao_publica/ibope_opiniao_publica_opp/ibope_opiniao_publica_opp_pd/ibope_opiniao_publica_opp_pd_pd_006_mr_0194.pdf

Both that URL and its [2017 archived snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20171206011842/http://segall.ifch.unicamp.br/documentos_digitalizados/ibope/ibope_opiniao_publica/ibope_opiniao_publica_opp/ibope_opiniao_publica_opp_pd/ibope_opiniao_publica_opp_pd_pd_006_mr_0194.pdf) failed web retrieval in this pass. Do not count the volume as accessible until fetched and inspected.

## What is not verified yet

- No continuous primary-source run for 1960–1963, 1981–1989 or 1990–1999 was established here. This is an access/coverage finding, not a claim those charts never existed.
- Retrospective Top 100 sites, blogs and Wikipedia can help discover issue dates, but should not substitute for the cited scan when accepting exact ranks.
- Nopem annual album best-seller lists cannot establish that every track on those albums was a charting song.
- Contemporary trade articles calling a song a “hit” are useful evidence of popularity but are not numeric chart positions. For example, Billboard 1992-09-12 discusses sertanejo hits (https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/Billboard/90s/1992/Billboard-1992-09-12.pdf); that article does not provide ranked positions for the named songs.

## Suggested ingestion boundary

Start with the verified Cash Box and Billboard issues, then expand outward only to issues whose chart headings are observed. Store publication date, observation date if printed, provider, market, chart type, position, complete original row, scan URL and PDF/printed page. Preserve grouped-performer rows as unresolved candidates. Review OCR aliases before Deezer matching. Chart year remains popularity evidence; derive the card year independently from the specific recording's original release.

## Direct-scan follow-up: 55 visually checked observations

Direct Python urllib downloads with a Mozilla User-Agent succeeded for four issues on 2026-11-15. The PDF pages were extracted with pypdf, rendered with pypdfium2 and visually inspected. `tools/songs/brazil/chart-seeds-historical.json` contains 55 weekly observations: 25 from Cash Box 1967-01-21 (printed78/PDF index77), ten from Billboard 1969-07-12 (printed69/index73), ten from Billboard 1975-04-26 (printed58/index93), and ten from Billboard 1980-09-20 (printed67/index74). PDF indices are zero-based.

These are Brazilian-market chart observations, not a verified Brazilian-artists-only list. Foreign artists are retained as raw evidence; nationality is explicitly unverified. Cash Box groups multiple performers under some ranks, so those observations remain review items. The 1975 scan prints Francisco Cusco, which is retained and flagged rather than silently normalized; Hyldon and Chrystian are legible in the scan and corrected relative to the search OCR. The 1969 scan identifies The Letter performer as Arbors (omitted by the indexed snippet). The 1980 scan prints Amelinha, resolving an earlier indexed misreading. No release years were inferred and no game catalog was changed.
